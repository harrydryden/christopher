# Production audit and hardening — 23 September 2026

AVA (formerly Christopher) was audited for correctness, concurrency, performance and robustness at a
target of 1,000 accounts, roughly 1,500 followed companies, 150,000 observed postings and 20,000 tasks a
day, on the deployment described in [docs/HOSTED-CAPACITY-2026-09-20.md](HOSTED-CAPACITY-2026-09-20.md):
a three-slot worker on a 512 MB instance, a three-connection pool per interface instance, and a 1 GB
database. This document is the register of what was found, what was changed, and what is deliberately
left for later. It supersedes nothing in [docs/SPEC.md](SPEC.md); where a decision here amends the
specification, the specification was amended too and the section is named.

## Method

Thirteen independent readers, one per lens, each read every file in its scope in full and reported
only what it had traced in the code, anchored to file and line: the task queue and scheduler; the scan
pipeline and change detection; outbound fetching and rendering; discovery and the ATS adapters; the
remaining task handlers; authentication and the public surface; the server actions; interface
performance; the schema, indexes and queries; the model engine and budgets; the CV pipeline; operations
and deployment; and the test infrastructure. Every batch of findings then went to two further readers:
a skeptic told to refute each finding from the code, and a lead reviewer told to assume it real and
decide severity, fix and blast radius. The organisation's spend limit stopped verification part-way, so
120 of the 236 findings carry a skeptic's verdict: 112 confirmed, 7 uncertain, 1 refuted. The three
critical findings were confirmed by hand. Every finding without a verdict was verified again by the
engineer who fixed it before any change was made, and those that did not hold are marked as such below.

The severity scale: **critical** is data loss, a cross-account leak, money spent wrongly, a false
"closed" role, or an outage at scale; **high** is user-visible wrong behaviour or a bottleneck that
fails at 1,000 accounts or 1,500 companies; **medium** and **low** follow. Where the skeptic corrected a
severity, the corrected value is shown.

## Headline

The system was already carefully built: leases with fencing, a per-account budget with holds, a shared
catalogue with per-account views, and 1,307 tests that were green before this work. The audit found no
cross-account data leak in the read paths and no way to spend another account's budget. What it found
instead falls into five groups.

1. **Two ways to break the closure rule.** A role reopened by a partial scan kept its miss counter, so
   one later miss closed it again; and nothing separated the two misses in time, so a manual rescan
   minutes after the daily one could close a role on a transient glitch. Both are fixed, and the rule
   now reads: absent from two consecutive successful scans at least six hours apart.
2. **Operational traps that would have bitten the next change.** The migration journal is dated a week
   ahead, so a migration generated before 30 September would have been skipped silently in production;
   the demo seed truncates whatever database it is pointed at; a mistyped CLI argument fanned out to
   the whole catalogue; every worker boot re-ran every account's gate.
3. **Cost that grows with accounts rather than with work.** Every scan rewrote every job and view
   whether or not anything changed, recompiled every follower's gate regexes per posting, and loaded the
   description text of every job the source had ever had; every daily run queued a snapshot re-parse
   per account; every settings save re-scored everything; pollers in every open tab refreshed whole
   pages every ten seconds through the daily run.
4. **Missing guards on the shared surfaces.** No SSRF guard on the URLs accounts supply; SVG logos
   served from the app origin without a content security policy; catalogue sources editable by any
   follower; a pasted posting on any host becoming a shared role; unbounded company and document
   submissions.
5. **Robustness under failure.** Handlers that outlive their deadline kept fetching, rendering and
   billing; pool errors with no listener; retention that deleted fewer rows an hour than arrived;
   verification budgets that dropped boards found late in a crawl; regexes that backtrack for minutes on
   a hostile page.

## Design decisions that shape the fixes

These are the decisions the fixes were built to, so that a later reader knows what was intended rather
than inferring it from the diff.

- **Closure needs independent evidence.** `jobs.first_missed_at` records when a role was first missed;
  closure requires the miss count to reach the threshold and at least six hours since that first miss.
  Being seen on any reconciling scan, partial included, resets both. (SPEC 3.4 and Appendix B amended.)
- **A retired source retires its roles.** When a source is disabled, superseded or left without a
  follower, its open roles close with `closed_at = last_seen_at` and a `source_retired` event. This is
  a lifecycle event a person or discovery caused, not a scan closure, and Appendix B says so.
- **Boot does not re-evaluate gates.** A version constant in `packages/core/src/tasks.ts`, compared
  with a stored value, decides whether a deploy re-runs the gate; only a gate semantics change bumps it.
- **Priority ageing has a floor.** Aged background work can tie with interactive work but never
  overtake it, and only work that is ready ages.
- **Handlers must stop when told.** The run's `AbortSignal` reaches the resource lease (which stops
  renewing), the fetcher, the browser and the model engine. Wiring every handler to it is the follow-up
  phase named below.
- **The catalogue is admin-edited, with one exception.** Confirming a discovery candidate for a company
  that has no confirmed source yet is allowed for any verified follower, because it completes the
  discovery they started. Everything else on a shared source is an administrator's.
- **A pasted posting on a foreign host is private.** It is stored for the pasting account alone; a
  posting on one of the company's known hosts becomes a shared role as before.
- **Public URLs only.** Every URL an account supplies, and every redirect it leads to, is resolved and
  refused if it points at a loopback, private, link-local or metadata address. The test host map is the
  one exception, because that is how the suites run.
- **Liveness is cheap.** `/healthz` answers from the process; the aggregate metrics move to `/status`,
  computed at most every thirty seconds and gated by a token.
- **Migrations are hand-dated and checked.** New journal entries must exceed the previous `when`; a
  test enforces it, and the migrator takes lock and statement timeouts.
- **The dedupe index covers queued work only.** An enqueue during a run yields one queued follow-up
  that sees the latest state, instead of being swallowed.
- **Members have a budget ceiling.** An account may raise its own monthly model budget to $100; an
  administrator may set any value.

## Deploy notes

- `SCRAPER_CONTACT_EMAIL` must be a real address on the worker (and on Vercel if `CRON_SECRET` is set); a
  production worker now refuses to start without one.
- Before deploying, compare production's `drizzle.__drizzle_migrations` with the journal (a read-only
  query). Boot now refuses on a historical gap, which is the point.
- Run the two `ALTER ROLE` statements in [DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md) and point the interface at Render's
  pooled URL (port 6432); the worker and migrations stay on the direct URL.
- Set `WORKER_STATUS_TOKEN` on the worker and in the release checks; `/healthz` no longer carries the
  metrics, `/status` does.
- Leave Render's "Docker Command" empty so the image's `tini` entrypoint is used.
- Old and new worker processes overlap briefly on a rolling deploy and use different advisory lock
  names since the rename; dedupe keys and existing-run checks still prevent duplicate scheduling.
- A signed-in administrator triggers the scheduler by POST from the site (the admin page's Run now
  control), not by visiting `/api/cron`.
- The closure-evidence migration ships as `0037` (after the `0036` main already applied) and the
  cost-sample index as `0038`; a migration numbered before an applied one is refused by the journal
  guard, which is what would have happened had it stayed `0035`.
- The first `rescore_all` pass after deploy re-scores each role once, because the scoring fingerprint
  now covers the evidence a role can use. `GATE_REEVALUATION_VERSION` was not bumped: the gate's
  decisions are unchanged. Bumping it to 2 would re-run every account once and repair views left
  archived by earlier widenings; that is an operator's call.
- Structured feeds may now spend 600 listing requests a scan; at the vendor hosts' 250 ms pacing that
  is about 150 s against the 3-minute scan deadline for a company with several very large boards.

## Deliberately deferred

These are real and recorded, and were left out of this round for the reason given.

- **Compiling the worker ahead of time.** tsx at runtime costs a loader thread and an esbuild child on
  a 512 MB instance. The trade-off is written in DEPLOY.md; a compiled build changes how migrations
  are located and deserves its own change.
- **DNS rebinding.** The fetcher resolves a name and then `fetch` resolves it again on connect, so a
  name that answers public then private can slip through. The fix is a connect-time address check
  through an undici agent, which needs undici as a direct dependency.
- **cheerio's parser is quadratic on unclosed formatting tags** (10,000 unclosed `<b>` tags take about
  38 seconds). It affects every `cheerio.load` on scraped HTML. Options are a pre-parse guard or
  htmlparser2 for link harvesting; changing the scan's parser changes what listings extract, so it
  needs its own evaluation.
- **Per-source newsletter tokens.** The inbound newsletter route authenticates with one deployment
  secret; a per-source token needs a schema column and issue/rotate actions. A limit of 50 documents
  per source per day was added instead.
- **Pruning `cv_libraries` and `preference_profiles`.** Library reviews are reused from older versions,
  and profile history needs a bounded listing first.
- **Signup still answers "exists"** for a registered address, because a new member is signed in at once
  and would learn the same fact.
- **The capacity benchmark** covers 100 users and 20 companies with no pollers; it should be extended
  to the polling and daily-run shapes this audit found.
- **Teamtailor and SuccessFactors verification markers** are marked *verify* in the specification and
  were not tightened without a real board to check against.

## Follow-up phase

Carried at integration, after the packages merged: every run's engine is bound to its stop signal, so
scan, discovery, import, CV and learning calls end with a deadline or a lost lease; the CV build,
library review and library import record each call against the hold it was admitted against, in one
transaction; the outage heuristic counts provider failures by the ledger's own definition; the
`workloadMetrics` query is restricted by status and month under a statement timeout; a retired source
stays off through a scheduled re-discovery and goes to Health for a person to decide; account deletion
runs under one lock and re-reads the caller; discovery's own URL filter applies the public-address
rule before a fetch is spent; expected refusals from the Learning, Companies and
Operations actions are read out on the page; the admin Accounts page reads one page of accounts; the
cost-per-build sample looks back ninety days.

The test-hardening package closed the test-infrastructure lens: every exported server action is
classified and refused without a session, every route handler answers 401 or 403, a second account
gets the not-found answer for another account's roles and documents, the suites truncate what they
assert over and count only their own locks, the web suites run on production's pool size, and the
SmartRecruiters case that failed under load was a fixture whose scans shared one frozen timestamp
and were told apart by whichever row the planner returned first. CI now runs the worker suite a
second time in a shuffled file order under a flipped query plan, which catches that whole class; a
tie audit found eighteen more scan fixtures with shared timestamps that pass today under both plans.
The catch-all that turns an action's error into a sentence no longer swallows a gate's refusal or
the redirect it asked for.

Still open: the capacity benchmark's new phases have not been run end to end against a dedicated
database, so its queries-per-second figure carries no threshold yet.
