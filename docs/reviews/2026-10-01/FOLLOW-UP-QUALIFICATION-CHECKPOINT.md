## New evidence

The [fresh Siemens public-HTTP audit](implementation-evidence/partial-publication/SIEMENS-LIVE-PUBLICATION.md) used the normal worker fetcher and host pacing in an isolated local database with a verified synthetic follower and deliberately broad, admit-all gate. After 20 pages and 50.330 seconds, 119 jobs and 119 account views were available before a terminal scan. A second 53.332-second claim reached 40 published pages and 239 jobs/views, with a queued continuation, unchanged source fingerprint, no `scans` row and no `last_ok_scan_at`. Read-only database evidence shows 239 distinct identities and `discovered` events, 239 seeded views, zero missing/closed rows and zero AI calls. These observations support the narrow claim that a large live listing can yield useful positives during its first two normally paced claims. The starting catalogue was empty, so zero closures do not test negative reconciliation. The gate's 239 views do not demonstrate personal relevance, posting precision or full Siemens coverage. The two-claim run does not measure sustained daily capacity or terminal completion.

The [raw claim JSON](implementation-evidence/partial-publication/live-siemens-claims.json) has a diagnostic `stagedPages` error: the initial harness counted expanded postings, reporting 119/239 in that field. The unchanged raw run, [corrected read-only query](implementation-evidence/partial-publication/live-siemens-readback.sql) and [database result](implementation-evidence/partial-publication/live-siemens-readback.json) distinguish this from the durable publication cursor and confirm 40 pages. The raw `requestCount` counts successful listing `fetchText` responses; `transferredBytes` is decoded HTML body size, not wire bytes. No additional external requests were made to fix those counters. The 29 September 20-page Siemens checkpoint staged 120 roles in 50.8 seconds with zero published jobs under the former behaviour; it is a different date and listing snapshot, not a controlled speed or recall comparison.

The [posting acceptance identity review](POSTING-ACCEPTANCE-IDENTITY-GATE-REVIEW.md) found a local reporter bug: two qualifying results for case A could replace a missing case B and produce a false pass. The reporter now rejects duplicate selected IDs, duplicate result IDs and unknown result IDs, and blocks acceptance when a selected case has no unique result. Observed wrong-source, posting-identity and exact-count failures remain failures even when another case is absent. The focused acceptance suites passed **51/51 tests across three files**, worker typecheck and the [worker build](implementation-evidence/acceptance-identity/worker-build.log) passed, and `git diff --check` passed. This validates the repaired accounting rules, not a new live posting-quality result. The historical 25-case report had 25 distinct matching IDs and was already blocked for missing posting labels and extraction evidence; there is no evidence this bug inflated that report.

The existing [partial-publication verification](PARTIAL-PUBLICATION-VERIFICATION.md) still supplies the local transaction, freshness, restart, browser and test evidence. The new public Siemens run adds one real host-pacing observation. Neither replaces an independently human-labelled, full-scope posting corpus or representative account-use evidence. The acceptance review found only machine-enumerated and stale or incomplete-scope posting references; it did not find a current qualifying human-reviewed corpus.

## First-use interface check

The [synthetic first-use and Health audit](implementation-evidence/firstuse-ux-audit/README.md) covered queued, interrupted and zero-result states at desktop and 375px phone widths. It found and repaired a Health sentence that incorrectly said an interrupted read had published no roles even when 25 matches were present. A queued scan had been called “Discovery queued” and sent the person to Companies without a stopped-worker instruction; Companies now calls it “Scan queued”, while setup explains the monitoring wait and links to Health. A stale running scan with a stopped worker had shown “Scanning…” and “Refreshing…”; Companies now says “Scan waiting for monitoring”, gives a keyboard-reachable Health link and keeps Refresh disabled with an honest waiting label. The worker query is limited to pending work in setup and visible running rows in Companies. The [fixed queued capture](implementation-evidence/firstuse-ux-audit/browser-queued-fixed.json) and running [before](implementation-evidence/firstuse-ux-audit/browser-running-offline-before.json) and [after](implementation-evidence/firstuse-ux-audit/browser-running-offline-fixed.json) captures show these states. Five focused web files passed **44/44 tests** ([log](implementation-evidence/firstuse-ux-audit/focused-tests.log)); [web typecheck](implementation-evidence/firstuse-ux-audit/typecheck.log) and the final [production web build](implementation-evidence/firstuse-ux-audit/web-build.log) passed. The browser recorded no page errors, external attempts or horizontal overflow, and the 375px Companies card and Health link were visually reviewed. The original queued pre-fix capture was overwritten by its fixed rerun; the audit records the pre-fix observation without presenting that capture as retained evidence. The current scripted checks do not substitute for unaided participants, native browser zoom or assistive-technology use.
